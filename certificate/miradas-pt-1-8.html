Frequently asked questions

How do I check the authenticity of this document?

To check it, verify these three points:

  • The artist’s signature and mark. The certificate must be signed by hand and carry the artist’s mark. The mark cannot be printed or reproduced in a photocopy.
  • Certificate number. It must match the one on this page, which you reach with the QR code.
  • Record on the Bitcoin blockchain. The work’s details (artist, title, year, technique and dimensions) were recorded through a digital fingerprint: a unique code that changes completely if a single character is altered. If any of those details were changed, the fingerprint would no longer match the recorded one. You can verify it here.
What is a blockchain?

A blockchain is a technology that records digital information securely and collectively, without relying on third parties or centralized servers.

Instead of being kept on one server, the information is copied across thousands of connected computers. Each new record is grouped with others into a block, and each block is linked to the previous one, forming a chain. Changing a record that has already been stored would require altering all the following blocks on most of those computers at the same time, which in practice is not possible.

The Bitcoin blockchain, in operation since 2009, was the first and remains the most widely used. The details of this work are recorded on that blockchain.

How is the blockchain record verified?

The record can be verified in two ways:

  • From this page. The Check button calculates, in your own browser, the digital fingerprint of the work’s details: a unique code that would change completely if any detail were ever modified. It then compares it with the one recorded on the blockchain, by querying four independent websites that publish its contents: Blockchain.com, Blockstream, Mempool, and BlockCypher.

Is the owner’s information public?

No. The blockchain record is public, but it does not include the owner’s name or any other personal data. It contains only a digital fingerprint, from which it is not possible to work out who the owner is.

That fingerprint is calculated from the owner’s name and a personal code that the artist writes by hand on the certificate. This way, only the owner and the artist can prove, if they wish, that the record belongs to the owner.
